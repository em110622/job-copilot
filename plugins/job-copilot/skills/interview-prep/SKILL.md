name: interview-prep
description: Prepare for an interview. Researches the company, the role, and the people you'll meet (professional public info only), predicts the likely questions, maps each to your strongest story or forløb, flags gaps, and suggests questions for you to ask. Saves a one-page brief to the application folder. Triggers on "prep me for my interview", "interview prep", "I have an interview with", "who am I meeting", or an interview invite.
---

## GUIDED DELIVERY - one part at a time (always)
Present in this order, each its own turn: (1) company and role, (2) the people, (3) likely questions and your answers, (4) your questions and logistics. Pause after (3) for gaps.

## FORMATTING - lists, never paragraphs (always)

---

Read `CLAUDE.md` (hard rules), `memory/profile.md`, `memory/experience.md`, `memory/stories.md`, `memory/contacts.md`, and the application folder (`posting.md`, `fit.md`, any earlier `debrief-*.md` for this company) first. If no application folder exists, create one as in `tailor-application` Step 0.

Ask what you don't know, in one batch: the date and format (phone screen, case, panel, final), who they are meeting (names and titles), and anything the recruiter said.

## Part 1 - Company and role
Research with web search and fetch (and the browser in `memory/tools.md` when a page needs it): what the company does and how it makes money, recent news (last ~6 months), strategy and challenges, the team the role sits in, and culture signals. Then the role: the real problem this hire solves, reading the posting and the research together. Five to eight bullets, each with a source link. Never state a current fact from memory alone.

## Part 2 - The people
For each interviewer: current role, background, time at the company, and public professional activity (posts, talks, articles), read via the browser if LinkedIn is used. Professional public information only. One line on what they will likely care about given their role. Add them to `memory/contacts.md` under "Interviewers met" only after the interview (interview-debrief does that).

## Part 3 - Likely questions and your answers
List 8 to 12 likely questions: the role-specific ones from the posting and research, the standard ones for this interview stage, and the awkward ones for this person (a gap in the CV, a short tenure, a career change, why leave now). For each:
- the best story from `memory/stories.md`, or the forløb to build one from;
- a one-line "headline" answer;
- the result to land, respecting publicity levels.

Flag gaps: questions with no good story. Offer to draft one from a forløb (and hand to `experience-coach` if the forløb is too thin), then save on yes to `memory/stories.md`.

## Part 4 - Your questions and logistics
Five sharp questions for the user to ask, tailored to each interviewer, drawn from the research. Then logistics: time, place or link, who to ask for, what to bring.

## Save
Write `research.md` in the application folder as a one-page brief (all four parts, links included). Update `posting.md`: `Status: interview` and `Next step: interview <date>`. Suggest a `mock-interview` session on the weakest three questions.
